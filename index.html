Research

My research spans three interlocking threads, each approaching language from a different angle of inquiry.

—01

Linguistic Structure in Neural Models

I probe large language models for evidence of syntactic and semantic representations, asking whether gradient-descent systems rediscover the categorical distinctions posited by linguistic theory. This work uses behavioral probing, representational similarity analysis, and targeted minimal-pair evaluations.

probing classifiers RSA minimal pairs
—02

Pragmatics & Discourse Grounding

I investigate how speakers coordinate on meaning beyond the literal: implicature, common ground, reference resolution. I am particularly interested in whether current NLP systems exhibit or merely simulate pragmatic competence, and what the difference between those cases might mean.

RST annotation studies behavioral exps.
—03

Cognitive Plausibility of Language Models

Bridging computational linguistics and cognitive science, I examine the degree to which language models constitute credible process models of human language processing — and the theoretical implications when they succeed or fail.

eye-tracking surprisal theory fMRI alignment
