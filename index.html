Surya Chinnappa

BA, University of Chicago

I am an incoming Ph.D. student at [pending], finishing up a B.A. in Linguistics and Cognitive Science with a minor in Computer Science at the University of Chicago.

My interests broadly concern the representation of meaning in minds and computational systems. Questions that arise in this area—what are the mental faculties underlying compositionality? How is the lexicon structured in the mind and brain? What aspects of meaning are inseparable from human experience and which can be made amodal?—lie at the borders of traditional disciplines. As such, while my disciplinary anchor is in linguistic theory, I integrate approaches from theoretical computer science and logic, psycholinguistics, and machine learning to address these issues from all angles.

My honors thesis, advised by Chris Kennedy, uses diachronic word embeddings to hunt for distributional signatures of a proposed law of unidirectional semantic change governing the metaphoric extension of synesthetic adjectives. Other ongoing work includes a text analysis project within the Compelling Preaching project at the Center for Practical Wisdom and APEX Lab (PI: Howard Nusbaum), and the development of a benchmark assessing LLMs' ability to recognize pragmatic felicity conditions and respond effectively to infelicitous utterances.

When I'm not thinking about words or talking about thinking, you'll likely find me making music—whether playing violin or any of a host of tiny flutes, singing sean-nós, or songwriting. Other hobbies include origami, constructed language, and calligraphy.
